# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

- **Patrons and prospective members** — affluent Lebanese and diaspora audiences who fund and join heritage causes. They arrive via word of mouth, galas, and press; they evaluate APSAD the way they evaluate an auction house or a five-star hotel: through the quality of its presentation.
- **Prospective volunteers** — students, professionals, and heritage enthusiasts looking for hands-on ways to contribute (fieldwork, archival research, outreach).
- **Institutional partners and press** — ministries, UNESCO-type bodies, foundations, and journalists using the site as APSAD's formal calling card.
- **The general public** — visitors learning about Lebanon's endangered cultural and natural heritage, often via search or the AI chatbot.

The job to be done: understand who APSAD is, trust the institution, and find a clear path to involvement (membership, volunteering, advocacy, partnership).

## Product Purpose

The digital home of APSAD (Association pour la Protection des Sites et Anciennes Demeures), the Lebanese NGO founded in 1960 that identifies, protects, preserves, and promotes Lebanon's cultural and natural heritage. The site presents the mission and six-decade history, showcases protected sites and projects through the gallery, converts visitors into members/volunteers/advocates via Get Involved and the contact form, and answers questions through an AI chatbot.

Success, in priority order: (1) visitors join — volunteer or become members; (2) public awareness of endangered Lebanese heritage grows; (3) institutions and press perceive APSAD as the authoritative guardian of Lebanese heritage.

## Brand Personality

**Warm, elegant, patrician.** The site speaks to an upper-class patronage audience — the tone of a cultural institution addressing benefactors as peers, never a charity pleading for help. Emotional goals: quiet confidence, reverence for the sites, the warmth of a living community behind the formality.

Reference points:
- **Luxury hospitality** (Aman Resorts, Belmond): photography-led, generous whitespace, serif elegance, quiet confidence for a wealthy clientele.
- **Cultural institutions** (Sursock Museum, Aga Khan Trust for Culture, World Monuments Fund): patronage-class heritage bodies with refined institutional presence.

## Anti-references

- **Generic charity template** — donate buttons everywhere, crisis photography, progress bars, guilt-driven appeals. Cheapens the brand for this audience.
- **Tourism-board gloss** — Visit-Lebanon brochure aesthetics, saturated postcard photos, "Explore!" energy. APSAD is a guardian, not a tour operator.
- **Dusty museum archive** — heavy academic walls of text, parchment textures, a site that feels like a 2009 PDF. Heritage does not mean old-fashioned.
- **Startup/SaaS modernism** — gradient buttons, rounded playful cards, tech-company grammar that undermines 60 years of institutional gravitas.

## Design Principles

1. **Patronage, not charity.** Invite visitors into a legacy; never plead. Elegance is the fundraising strategy — the site's refinement is what earns upper-class trust.
2. **Let the monuments speak.** Photography of Baalbek, Byblos, and the protected sites carries the emotional load. The interface frames the imagery; it never competes with it.
3. **Sixty years of authority.** Every surface should read as established and institutional. Credibility precedes conversion — a visitor believes in APSAD before being asked to join.
4. **Warmth within formality.** Community custodianship keeps the elegance human. People and stories temper the grandeur so refinement never turns cold.
5. **Craft is the proof point.** A preservation society's own site must itself feel impeccably preserved — precision in typography, spacing, and motion signals the same care APSAD applies to stone.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.1 AA** is the committed bar: ≥4.5:1 body-text contrast (watch muted gold and warm grays on the light background), full keyboard navigation, screen-reader semantics.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` is already honored globally and must remain so as motion work expands (the site is motion-rich: split text, parallax, marquee, Ken Burns, custom cursor).
- Audience is trilingual in practice (English, French, Arabic — APSAD's own name is French). English-only today; copy and layout choices should not preclude future French/Arabic (RTL) support.
